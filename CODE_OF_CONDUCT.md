# Code of conduct

This is one person's portfolio site. The contributor base is one person and a
handful of people who might open an issue about a broken link. The rules are
short because the situation is small — not because conduct does not matter.

## What is expected

Be civil. Argue with the code, the copy, or the decision, not with the person
who wrote it. Assume the other party is acting in good faith until they show
you otherwise.

Say what you actually mean. A terse review comment is fine; a dismissive one is
not, and the difference is whether it tells the reader what to do next.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks, in issues, pull requests, commit
  messages, or anywhere else this project reaches.
- Comments about a person's race, nationality, religion, gender, sexuality,
  disability, or appearance.
- Sexual language or imagery.
- Publishing anyone's private information — an address, a phone number, a
  private email — without their explicit permission. This project takes that
  seriously enough to have a written rule about it: see `CLAUDE.md` §2.8–2.10
  on why no third party's contact details are ever committed here.
- Sustained disruption: reopening settled arguments, brigading a thread, or
  filing issues to make a point rather than to fix something.

## Scope

This applies in the repository — issues, pull requests, discussions, commits —
and to anyone representing the project elsewhere.

## Reporting

Email **najiyaffan@gmail.com**. Reports go to me and nobody else. Tell me what
happened and where; you do not have to argue that it was serious enough.

I will read every report and respond. Depending on what happened, the outcome
may be a private word, an edited or deleted comment, or a block. If the report
is about me, say so plainly in the email — there is no committee to escalate
to here, and pretending otherwise would be dishonest.

Nobody is penalised in good standing for reporting something in good faith.

## Attribution

This is written for this repository rather than adopted from a template. If you
want the widely used standard instead, it is the
[Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/) — a fuller
document, with enforcement guidelines suited to projects that have more than
one maintainer.
